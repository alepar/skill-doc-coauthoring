# Dossier: Vendor style-steering guidance and existing humanizer / de-slop tools

## Summary

Three vendors now publish what amounts to an admission list of their own models' default tics, and a small ecosystem of open-source "humanizer" skills, samplers and scorers has grown up around the same patterns. Read together, the two bodies of evidence give /unclaude three things: a vendor-confirmed seed list of tells (sycophantic openers, over-formatting, preamble, caveat bloat, mannered prose, sincerity intensifiers, contrastive framing, slop vocabulary); a set of architectural moves that the better tools converge on (audit before rewrite, strong-versus-weak-alone tiering, protected contexts, a writing-sample override, a second scan for tells that survive the rewrite, a no-fabrication contract); and a well-documented catalogue of what goes wrong when the fixing step is crude (meaning drift, invented specifics, quote tampering, tortured phrases, staccato fragmentation, a new "humanizer voice", content loss, register drift). The strongest single finding for the design is that detection is not the bottleneck: the rewrite step decides the quality of the outcome, and every humanizer audited degrades text to some degree. That argues for many cheap, narrow scouts and one careful, constrained fixer with explicit "never inject" rules and a verification pass.

Evidence strength is mixed. Vendor documentation (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google) is authoritative about what each vendor considers a defect but contains no measurements. Peer-reviewed or preprint work (Antislop, DAMAGE, tortured phrases, LAMP, DIPPER, Russell et al., Jakesch et al.) is the strongest evidence on failure modes and on banlist limits. The humanizer skills themselves are practitioner artefacts: their rules are design opinions, valuable mainly as converged practice.

## Facet Questions and Scope

- What failure modes are documented for humanizers and banlists (DAMAGE audit, Pangram's slop-squared analysis, tortured phrases, the humanizer stress test, banlist whack-a-mole, the pink-elephant effect)?

Out of scope here: the per-pattern statistics for individual tells (dashes, contrast frames, vocabulary), which other facets cover in depth; they appear below only where a tool or vendor document speaks to them.

## Source Groups and Findings

### A. Vendor steering guidance as a confession of default tics

**Anthropic (vendor docs, system prompts).** Anthropic's system prompts have named specific opener tics for years. The Claude 3.5 Sonnet-era prompt told the model to respond without affirmations like "Certainly!", "Of course!", "Absolutely!", "Great!", "Sure!" [54]. <!-- claim: 833754474756b317; evidence: de31d105aee66816; source: f493d610463286dc --> The Opus 4.1 prompt added a ban on praising the question: Claude never starts by calling a question or idea good, great, fascinating, profound or excellent [39]. <!-- claim: b0ad965600caab3a; evidence: cbebfa91a128ca0d; source: f85803be64a102f8 --> The sycophantic-agreement tic became public enough that an Anthropic staff member replied on the Claude Code issue tracker with a joke reply and a stopgap hook [37]. <!-- claim: e9925f73d419731c; evidence: 78101550ff66a431; source: cdc616fae4f332cc --> By 2026 the Fable 5.1 system prompt bans three sincerity intensifiers outright: "genuinely", "honestly" and "straightforward" [51]. <!-- claim: 3c74df31eaef5a71; evidence: b9cbb525c49a25c1; source: b08f5506b2c43068 --> The Opus 5.5 system prompt still carries an anti-over-formatting rule against bold, headers, lists and bullets [52]. <!-- claim: dd0b875f13b2672b; evidence: 4491995768631720; source: 3e045f52520583bf -->

The developer prompting guide gives positive-form fixes rather than bans. For long-form content it asks for flowing prose paragraphs, reserving markdown for code and simple headings and avoiding bold and italics [45]. <!-- claim: 6fe69229860dd50c; evidence: 96d687a4a0ec0bb8; source: da45de5646cbbc5b --> It explicitly recommends rephrasing "Do not use markdown" as a positive description of the desired prose [45]. <!-- claim: 48f3a6f75716822a; evidence: fb5f951581506479; source: da45de5646cbbc5b --> It also notes register mirroring: prompt formatting bleeds into output formatting [45]. <!-- claim: f64b0072f9adee95; evidence: b8965cd2fba1f112; source: da45de5646cbbc5b --> For preamble it suggests naming the openers directly ("Here is...", "Based on...") [45]. <!-- claim: d863cbb0e3b960e7; evidence: 07833b574fd610b3; source: da45de5646cbbc5b --> The same guide tells Claude that it tends toward generic "on distribution" outputs, which is the vendor's own account of where "AI slop" comes from [45]. <!-- claim: 9a51da946db27650; evidence: c5bff08c8d434571; source: da45de5646cbbc5b -->

Model-specific notes in the guide document generation drift. Opus 5 runs longer by default, and effort settings do not fix visible length [45]. <!-- claim: 7caabc5038d9dae0; evidence: ee5a2e04a355107a; source: da45de5646cbbc5b --> Fable 5.1 has fewer stock phrases but denser prose with long sentences and few paragraph breaks, and the guide says a defined anti-pattern instruction helps [46]. <!-- claim: 2983be06bddd82d0; evidence: 9c63680261d4ecfd; source: 557f96ccb3270d91 --> That anti-pattern is "mannered prose": metaphor and flourish substituted for direct statement, as in "a dial worth turning" for "a parameter worth varying" [46]. <!-- claim: 1880a6c7585304b4; evidence: 76b50f148ebf6ba7; source: 557f96ccb3270d91 --> For Opus 5.5 the guide observes that a vague instruction such as "avoid a generic AI look" swaps one default for another, while naming specific patterns works [50]. <!-- claim: 5d85e3ece68b82ec; evidence: 1ca3e84d4b6aa777; source: 72a68f793144bdd2 --> The Sonnet 5 guide warns that long-form prose style shifts with every release and that style prompts need re-evaluation [49]. <!-- claim: 157f6cd6a426aff3; evidence: 94c5fdf76aadd8e8; source: 1b5309285f0ed921 --> Across these guides Anthropic prefers positive examples of the desired style over instructions about what not to do [47]. <!-- claim: 74f3c566c5126ee3; evidence: 660c0be001b7e5fa; source: 545654c2e6e4a55e -->

Practitioner measurement corroborates the drift. Graphite reports that contextual-helpfulness phrases ("can help you", "is especially helpful") grew from Opus 5 to Opus 5.5 [30]. <!-- claim: edf640c56feecb0d; evidence: 5fcd4a08e7ce8c87; source: ffb7b5602c519659 -->

**OpenAI (vendor docs, Model Spec).** OpenAI's GPT-6 guidance is the most explicit vendor slop list found: "Bottom Line:", delve, foster, leverage, "it's worth noting", importantly, the "Question? Answer." device, "This isn't about X. It's about Y.", genuinely, and hyphenated compound descriptions [55]. <!-- claim: 17862aa4a3d28dab; evidence: 0b94d79f6a8a2e58; source: 5c4b950aaeff3364 --> The same guide forbids unprompted "X, not Y" contrastive framing and invented compound labels, vague qualifiers and canned transitions [55]. <!-- claim: 498b2579ebcbcae9; evidence: 6a729a5653013914; source: 5c4b950aaeff3364 --> It describes the model's own default as detailed and formatted, with recurring phrases across sessions [55]. <!-- claim: cf497128a3d79435; evidence: f05600f26c49e6e0; source: 5c4b950aaeff3364 --> The Model Spec states the general norm: avoid purple prose, hyperbole, self-aggrandizing language and clichéd phrases, and use formatting judiciously [56]. <!-- claim: 7732d9a8ad892430; evidence: 38537fff9afafd9f; source: 9f6def883554b727 --> It also names excessive hedging, disclaimers, apologies and "as an AI" reminders as defects [56]. <!-- claim: c13961e55174c7e0; evidence: 0f41fad013868965; source: 9f6def883554b727 --> OpenAI exposes a `verbosity` API parameter that controls final-answer length [57]. <!-- claim: dbcec3878159d6b6; evidence: 00e102d05d4f5fe5; source: 643282b96ef2f98c -->

**Google (vendor docs).** Google's Gemini 3 guide says the model is less verbose by default and prefers direct, efficient answers [59]. <!-- claim: e3a1514501548703; evidence: 6daa935d14157fd7; source: 15e2671c3bef9633 --> That makes Gemini the opposite pole from Opus 5 on length. The Gemini documentation is otherwise thin on named tics; it steers by few-shot examples rather than banlists.

**What the vendor lists reveal.** Read as a set, the vendor lists confirm a shared core of tells across families (opener flattery and affirmation, preamble, over-formatting, caveat and disclaimer bloat, contrastive "not X but Y" framing, slop vocabulary) and some family-specific ones: sincerity intensifiers and mannered prose for Claude, hyphenated compound labels and "Bottom Line:" summaries for GPT, terseness rather than bloat for Gemini. Practitioner data agree that family signatures diverge: Graphite finds 65% of tells unique to one family [29]. <!-- claim: eecf22fd89171231; evidence: 4771c30b1c94eed6; source: 56493642657ef5f7 --> The same study finds 55% to 72% of tells unique to one version even between the two latest releases of a family [29]. <!-- claim: 5f2a2242dee76ec3; evidence: eff9b1081d871fd1; source: 56493642657ef5f7 -->

**Prompt-level bans are unreliable.** Sam Altman publicly celebrated GPT-5.1 finally obeying a no-em-dash custom instruction, but Ars Technica found user experience still varied [62]. <!-- claim: be30bc39531001b9; evidence: a4ec9d7af1956143; source: bf568d6403c2ac98 --> A controlled study finds that even explicit em-dash prohibition fails to eliminate the artifact in some models [28]. <!-- claim: 8944465611908908; evidence: bf066e6e78604e90; source: 0bbcac4a1b3d1437 --> Under direct prohibition, GPT-4.1 still produced 3.86 em dashes per 1,000 words and DeepSeek V3 produced 1.57 [28]. <!-- claim: 6b7d0137f0dee971; evidence: dc3e9b4c584fe0a7; source: 0bbcac4a1b3d1437 --> The LAMP study likewise found clichés in every other response despite a prompt explicitly asking the models to avoid them [100]. <!-- claim: dd3db4d1e924109f; evidence: 266b7a08f053a076; source: 604a33a5ff2aff54 --> Inference: a post-hoc editing pass is needed even when generation-time prompts are tuned, which is the premise of /unclaude.

### B. Tool-by-tool pattern catalogues and architecture

**blader/humanizer (practitioner, Claude skill).** Humanizer ranks its patterns by strength: §1 to §5 justify an edit on a single sighting, while patterns marked "weak alone" need other tells in the same passage [64]. <!-- claim: 9f64b3e8f2fca75d; evidence: ddb1a657d7531489; source: 143979a9b24d1841 --> It leads with structure because word habits change with each release while structural habits persist [64]. <!-- claim: e3a5a9ac847d5a4a; evidence: ef3e598207f08526; source: 143979a9b24d1841 --> Its workflow ends with a second scan for the tells that most often survive a rewrite: contrasts, closers, triads, dashes and bold labels [64]. <!-- claim: cc591786e38f4822; evidence: 313cf595599264c1; source: 143979a9b24d1841 --> A user writing sample overrides the pattern rules, including the dash rule, which becomes "match the sample's rate" [64]. <!-- claim: efe00f2796da0d51; evidence: 60336dbbc35deec5; source: 143979a9b24d1841 --> The fixer is bound by a content contract: keep every supported claim, and add no fact, name, number, date, quote or citation that did not come from the source or the user [64]. <!-- claim: 2bfb59b4d7390c2c; evidence: 589cfe904ca2ae16; source: 143979a9b24d1841 --> It tells the fixer to restate each point naturally rather than patch flagged phrases one at a time [64]. <!-- claim: d298d0c1271c3783; evidence: 7972d5c098214aa5; source: 143979a9b24d1841 --> It protects quotations, titles, proper names and passages that mention a phrase rather than use it [64]. <!-- claim: 5b3a4cfa698d142f; evidence: 6b3a36c256f76f8f; source: 143979a9b24d1841 --> It disclaims detector evasion: the goal is human readers, and detectors still flag most of its output [3]. <!-- claim: 6935c29b9e2cb0a5; evidence: 63bcffd90af9ed39; source: 2db4f7d2d7de0ea2 --> Its README reports a 16-of-16 blind-preference win over the original AI text [3]. <!-- claim: 281b8f22cd6db758; evidence: 045250a6944143ce; source: 2db4f7d2d7de0ea2 --> That figure is self-reported and unreplicated.

**stop-slop (practitioner).** stop-slop is the aggressive end of the spectrum. Its first rule removes throat-clearing openers, emphasis crutches and every adverb [65]. <!-- claim: a415d210017926cf; evidence: 6fdb7f15522ba938; source: 041055887f823eb7 --> Its rhythm rule says "Two items beat three" and "No em dashes" [65]. <!-- claim: 38ecd8950efaa56c; evidence: 4ffb127c4269aaa7; source: 041055887f823eb7 --> It scores drafts on five dimensions and sends anything below 35/50 back for revision [65]. <!-- claim: ce8a540286d10caa; evidence: 2c8ca1a8f26db3d3; source: 041055887f823eb7 --> Its structural catalogue includes useful entries the vendor lists lack, such as "false agency", where inanimate things get human verbs so that the actor goes unnamed [65]. <!-- claim: 4d18f72fa928b599; evidence: 78ebc024e6c1bc0b; source: 041055887f823eb7 --> Inference: blanket adverb deletion and a hard two-not-three rule are over-correction risks. They produce a recognisable "anti-slop" register of short, adverb-free sentences in pairs.

**unslop (practitioner).** unslop is the most conservative design and the closest to what /unclaude needs from its fixer. It audits first and rewrites only on request [66]. <!-- claim: 4d410bca13bfc751; evidence: bce00cbf72647fd5; source: 8870b50ae0ada072 --> Its contract states that punctuation, vocabulary, cadence and structure alone prove neither a defect nor authorship [66]. <!-- claim: 7593450baefdcbe7; evidence: 911e1ec16f07fbfd; source: 8870b50ae0ada072 --> A scanner match never authorizes an edit by itself, and literal, domain-valid, quoted, attributed or genre-natural uses are protected [66]. <!-- claim: 1f5f492bcef0ae0e; evidence: fc25f23c966ebffc; source: 8870b50ae0ada072 --> The rewrite touches only sentences with confirmed findings, copies all other sentences byte-for-byte, and returns the source unchanged if nothing is confirmed [66]. <!-- claim: 1d5a4b2d15f821ae; evidence: 9545baa254e7e4b8; source: 8870b50ae0ada072 --> It forbids adding claims, advice, personality, anecdote, certainty or conclusions, and forbids staccato anti-slop prose [66]. <!-- claim: 47709918aecd5105; evidence: b0d55d9c7777a8d6; source: 8870b50ae0ada072 --> It preserves force-bearing "never", "must" and "all" in safety, security, legal and technical rules [66]. <!-- claim: a76e23183bb85aef; evidence: bc6ee2140a4add33; source: 8870b50ae0ada072 -->

**deslop (practitioner, scientific writing).** deslop adds a domain keep-list: in scientific writing, technical terminology is precise language and should stay, while business buzzwords and AI vocabulary leaking into technical prose are the target [67]. <!-- claim: 9fc2b0017884cf1c; evidence: 7085855a870f6a5a; source: 171c8455dd272c61 -->

**avoid-ai-writing (practitioner).** avoid-ai-writing contributes the clearest statement of fixer-side failure. Asking a model to "put voice back" makes it reach for a stock kit of human moves and install a personality the author never had [68]. <!-- claim: dea0820b70f2d7c3; evidence: f5248d6aca0161d6; source: 10ac1554017e831d --> Invented specifics are the most tempting fix, and a fabricated specific is worse than the vague phrase it replaced, so the gap should be flagged, never filled [68]. <!-- claim: c3924c83d5473a99; evidence: ba2c43e755752063; source: 10ac1554017e831d --> These rules constrain the editor rather than detect anything in the text, because the difference between an author's aside and an inserted one is provenance, which no pattern can see [68]. <!-- claim: fc911108c39201c3; evidence: 887b46069e2906f7; source: 10ac1554017e831d --> A rewrite that clears every flag but erases the source's cadence, stance or idiosyncrasies counts as a failure [68]. <!-- claim: 69cf5c36532014c7; evidence: 70f036815480c605; source: 10ac1554017e831d -->

**Claudisms (practitioner banlist).** Claudisms is a living catalogue of overused phrases, verbs and sentence shapes, each with a note on why it fails and what to write instead [125]. <!-- claim: 00f4e13b0cf3f954; evidence: 422d95e38dc4d30c; source: d6422a35628292a4 --> The "why it fails / write instead" format is directly reusable as fix guidance per finding.

**AntiSlop sampler and Antislop framework (code plus preprint).** The AntiSlop sampler works at generation time: it backtracks and retries with adjusted token probabilities when it hits a banned word or phrase [69]. <!-- claim: dd47a9f83826538e; evidence: b74ddbaa3b820c46; source: 722f0b87f10a5699 --> It can also enforce regex constraints such as banning "not x, but y" [69]. <!-- claim: 7d2573ca25e191be; evidence: 9227f92de32666d4; source: 722f0b87f10a5699 --> Its maintainer warns that the default list is mostly auto-generated from an LLM story dataset, uncurated, and that users should roll their own [69]. <!-- claim: e9b4320488b2a7da; evidence: d810c6ce7934655e; source: 722f0b87f10a5699 --> Per-token logit biasing fails because most slop phrases span several tokens, and down-weighting partial-word tokens damages output [69]. <!-- claim: dd49785ccf8f0e1f; evidence: 0e82ea5d022b1e09; source: 722f0b87f10a5699 --> The Antislop paper reports patterns more than 1,000 times over-represented in LLM output, and says the sampler suppresses 8,000+ patterns while token banning becomes unusable at 2,000 [12]. <!-- claim: b98ae576de090ca8; evidence: b563474ee43b3454; source: 96aebd41d7aadaa7 --> The paper states that telling a model to avoid banned vocabulary has limited efficacy and may backfire through the "pink elephant" problem [12]. <!-- claim: d086712cabc40207; evidence: 38cd32741ae430b9; source: 96aebd41d7aadaa7 --> Its automated pipeline re-runs extra iterations to catch slop that emerges after the first set is banned [12]. <!-- claim: 818428715336a662; evidence: 5ebe3b547c8dc03e; source: 96aebd41d7aadaa7 --> This is the whack-a-mole effect in its own documentation. It also finds that slop fingerprints cluster within model families and differ between them [12]. <!-- claim: 3dfe3cb0ab30ddbe; evidence: 902db5259bd33a49; source: 96aebd41d7aadaa7 -->

**Slop Score (EQ-Bench, practitioner metric).** Slop Score weights slop words at 60%, not-x-but-y contrast patterns at 25% and slop trigrams at 15% [13]. <!-- claim: 6e63d3eb0857f6e0; evidence: 698fdd2d6f20dd2f; source: d7571288888b16cd --> It explicitly does not classify text as AI or human; it flags glaringly over-used patterns [13]. <!-- claim: b858ae028f8f68c3; evidence: ae4810fbdda62bbc; source: d7571288888b16cd --> It is tuned for creative writing and essays and will likely under-report elsewhere [13]. <!-- claim: 87cb3c6ef2ca0c22; evidence: eeb02244305b241d; source: d7571288888b16cd -->

### C. Documented failure modes of humanizers and banlists

**DAMAGE audit (preprint).** DAMAGE examined 19 humanizer and paraphrasing tools for faithfulness to the original meaning [74]. <!-- claim: 8ea27fa4173feed5; evidence: a3a7aaa12d7dc21f; source: 01c098d2d5f066bc --> LLM-based humanizers drift further from the source, while synonym-swapping rule-based tools stay closer to it [74]. <!-- claim: 5df4a6f17f39b5c8; evidence: fcc669170e43f837; source: 01c098d2d5f066bc --> Every humanizer degraded the original to some degree, though by varying amounts [74]. <!-- claim: 0aa81c8a1d18de7b; evidence: 588adffa9c16e8ca; source: 01c098d2d5f066bc --> One failure type splits every sentence into single-clause statements [74]. <!-- claim: 0d834e5aa94c788c; evidence: 126338b05a6248b0; source: 01c098d2d5f066bc --> The better LLM-based tools adopt the source's writing level and tone rather than imposing one [74]. <!-- claim: 53fe6423154a980d; evidence: b3997833bc4fa875; source: 01c098d2d5f066bc -->

**Pangram, "slop squared" (vendor of a detector; industry report).** Pangram argues that humanizers make AI text less human, less comprehensible and less accurate [75]. <!-- claim: 7b22d27e9346e964; evidence: 8f35ec456ff0584a; source: 3b957664240fc36e --> Its specific cases map onto fixer constraints. One tool removed every hyphen across 164 occurrences, turning "multi-ethnic inner-city street" into "multi, ethnic inner, city street" [75]. <!-- claim: df468adf3b0a4a00; evidence: e37fcf84c5a079e1; source: 3b957664240fc36e --> Another edited direct quotations while presenting them as genuine [75]. <!-- claim: 8d327117e03591fe; evidence: c21c2beea62175a8; source: 3b957664240fc36e --> One shifted "youth" to "youngster" in a serious passage [75]. <!-- claim: 86755f433d519af0; evidence: 8e7a1503cbe1525c; source: 3b957664240fc36e --> Another produced a meaning change, rewriting "the male-dominated field of aviation" as "the previously masculine-dominated field of aviation" [75]. <!-- claim: 6b9789b982c6c618; evidence: 61ce7a5cf4419eee; source: 3b957664240fc36e --> Pangram sells a detector, so its framing is interested, but the examples are concrete.

**Tortured phrases (preprint).** Tortured phrases are odd substitutes for established terms, such as "counterfeit consciousness" for "artificial intelligence" [73]. <!-- claim: 85e2d6e78dcb8a9d; evidence: ffd89d2e4b80b62b; source: 1f19853b9e98661a --> They arise from word-by-word synonym substitution on a multi-word term [73]. <!-- claim: b6409b6c33e88e97; evidence: 6870b0dc7623abdd; source: 1f19853b9e98661a --> Inference: any lexical-replacement step in the fixer must treat multi-word technical terms as atomic.

**Humanizer stress test (practitioner).** A stress test of blader/humanizer found seven of ten rewrites shorter than the original, with substantive content lost in six [76]. <!-- claim: b791c1fae142b9d4; evidence: 662cb8c4775ea0c6; source: 3c875bce4cfa4dff --> In one case it invented a historical claim about 1970s AI research anxiety [76]. <!-- claim: 3181fbba4af23cbd; evidence: 7568b5b6af1093cf; source: 3c875bce4cfa4dff --> It swapped one fingerprint for another: abstractions and "However" pivots became fragments, shrug closers and one-sentence paragraphs stacked like index cards [76]. <!-- claim: b03df68744f36878; evidence: 5476f6d1c7a5eb15; source: 3c875bce4cfa4dff --> The author concluded that detection scores are decoupled from publishability [76]. <!-- claim: 69a253199e0c918f; evidence: 4fac2261d31b2af5; source: 3c875bce4cfa4dff --> The test is a single author's informal run, and the tested version is not stated in the evidence; Humanizer v3.1.0's content contract (above) appears designed to block exactly these failures.

**Iterated LLM editing adds AI traits (peer-reviewed).** Russell et al. abandoned a multi-call LLM humanization pipeline because each call added more AI characteristics [84]. <!-- claim: 4f1746a34e944cdf; evidence: 4ef54be080b8bd6c; source: 94bc1872a6410141 --> The LAMP study found LLM editors sometimes replace one cliché with another, and that adding specific detail is the hardest edit category [100]. <!-- claim: 68d3aa93addd346d; evidence: 54caae6b3e249a72; source: 604a33a5ff2aff54 --> Its strongest result for architecture is that oracle spans and automatically detected spans gave near-identical outcomes, so the rewriting step, not detection, is the bottleneck [100]. <!-- claim: 8f2af96033cda48b; evidence: c87b4439a03588ef; source: 604a33a5ff2aff54 -->

**Detector games are a separate problem.** DIPPER paraphrasing drops DetectGPT accuracy from 70.3% to 4.6% with little semantic change [71]. <!-- claim: cb74a0ee0d4b2436; evidence: 96aef74f2c48e4cc; source: 83e70f1452f01d94 --> Jakesch et al. show that AI text can exploit flawed human heuristics to be judged "more human than human" [87]. <!-- claim: 9ead5a7406f6ba02; evidence: 455ec5f77fdd10d5; source: 1c828b523d9d3c8f --> Inference: detector scores are neither a fix target nor a quality signal for /unclaude; reader-facing quality is.

## Counterevidence and Disagreements

- **Mechanical stripping removes voice.** A practitioner critique of the Wikipedia list argues that stripping every em dash, triplet and strong statement removes the parts of a draft with any life in them [5]. <!-- claim: e0d57bb7333fc947; evidence: 44c8c2320da40ae6; source: c3e630563d972c51 --> It also notes that some Wikipedia tells exist because of Wikipedia's neutral-point-of-view rule, and marketing copy is supposed to take a position [5]. <!-- claim: 9e5896f1d8336ffe; evidence: 872be879639c221f; source: c3e630563d972c51 --> This directly contradicts stop-slop's blanket rules.
- **Some vendor-banned moves are legitimate product voices.** Anthropic's own Opus 4.8 guide gives "acknowledge the user's framing before answering" as a valid tone instruction [48]. <!-- claim: 86eb45b125e1a250; evidence: b6f98a36d12dd1f6; source: cb3cf15d3617cd77 --> An acknowledgement opener is therefore a tell only relative to the requested register.
- **Over-steering carries forward across generations.** Anthropic warns that anti-formatting blocks written for earlier models can suppress structure Fable 5.1 actually needs [45]. <!-- claim: d8e02a7fbdfede70; evidence: 4e2fe163fb3915a9; source: da45de5646cbbc5b --> OpenAI similarly warns that "be concise" on a newer model can make answers too brief [55]. <!-- claim: f0b4098b67913706; evidence: b85f650f8097b83a; source: 5c4b950aaeff3364 -->
- **Hedges are not always filler.** Proofed notes that a hedge often covers an unverified claim, so deleting it blindly overstates confidence [126]. <!-- claim: ae543ca6360db279; evidence: 2aac88e3fb04be13; source: 2d868d4de107c27b --> This conflicts with stop-slop's "no hedges" rule and supports unslop's and Humanizer's narrower treatment.
- **Co-occurrence over single sightings.** Proofed's editors hold that an occasional em dash or triad is fine and co-occurrence is the problem [126]. <!-- claim: 26c23eab954988ae; evidence: 75fb359146c87299; source: 2d868d4de107c27b --> That supports weak-alone tiering but conflicts with Humanizer's own hard rule that the final text carries no dashes absent a sample.
- **Few targeted bans beat many.** A practitioner reading of the Wikipedia guide advises prompting out only a few patterns, because many restrictions make output stiff [4]. <!-- claim: 0661e63844c4cf32; evidence: 5b23c5584cfbb00d; source: 5cce2b8dd13a00d9 -->
- **Banlists chase a moving target.** Signalbox argues that tics have moved from n-grams to constructions, so n-gram catalogues and prompt banlists stop working [30]. <!-- claim: e9504eb9543bcb64; evidence: ed9c457277de2cdb; source: ffb7b5602c519659 --> Its core finding is that Opus 5.5 relocates AI-ness into the task's register rather than reducing it [30]. <!-- claim: 43918356b85368d1; evidence: b04f4f8bcb1dac86; source: ffb7b5602c519659 -->
- **Paraphrase need not wreck quality.** Sadasivan et al. report recursive paraphrasing that only slightly degrades text quality in many cases [72]. <!-- claim: 54e280f75a0e11f0; evidence: 98206d199eef8358; source: bb58e1dce57ecd0d --> This tempers the "all humanizers destroy text" reading; the degradation is a property of crude tools and unconstrained rewriting, not of rewriting itself.

## Methodological Limits and Open Gaps

- The humanizer skills (Humanizer, stop-slop, unslop, deslop, avoid-ai-writing) have no published evaluations beyond Humanizer's self-reported 16/16 preference result and one informal stress test. Their rules are converged practice, not validated interventions.
- DAMAGE and Pangram audited commercial detector-evasion humanizers, which optimise for detector scores. Their failure rates may overstate the risk for a reader-quality editor that has a no-fabrication contract, but the failure types are the right checklist.
- Vendor documents state intent, not measurement. They show what each vendor considers a defect at a given date; they do not show prevalence.
- Antislop's frequency data and Slop Score are weighted toward creative fiction; their slop lists are a poor fit for technical or business prose without re-derivation.
- No source evaluates a scout-then-fixer split against a single-pass editor directly. LAMP's detection-is-not-the-bottleneck result is the closest evidence, and it comes from literary fiction.
- Model-generation drift (55% to 72% of tells version-specific) means any static lexical catalogue will need regular refresh; structural patterns are the stable core.

## Proposed lane material

**Design lessons for /unclaude (scout lanes + single fixer)**

1. *Scouts report, never edit.* Adopt unslop's audit-first rule. Each scout returns findings with span, pattern id, tier (strong / weak-alone), and a one-line reason; nothing is rewritten in the scout phase.
2. *Strong vs weak-alone tiering in the consolidated report.* Strong tells (contrast frames, one-line restating closers, staged run-ups, arguing with no one, sycophantic openers, preamble, knowledge-limit disclaimers) are actionable on one sighting. Weak-alone tells (single dash, single triad, single hedge, a single listed word) are actionable only when the consolidator sees two or more distinct tells in the same paragraph. The consolidator, not the scouts, applies the co-occurrence threshold, because only it sees all lanes.
3. *Writing-sample override.* If the user supplies a sample of their own prose, the consolidator suppresses findings for features the sample uses at a similar rate (dashes, triads, contractions, parentheticals, sentence length). Without a sample, use rate ceilings rather than zero rules.
4. *Protected contexts, checked before reporting.* Quotations, titles, proper names, code and inline code, paths and URLs, mentions-not-uses, salutations and sign-offs, domain terminology, and force-bearing "never/must/all" in rules. A dedicated "protect" pre-pass that marks these spans for all scouts is cheaper than having every lane re-derive them.
5. *Single fixer with a hard contract.* Edit only confirmed findings; copy everything else byte-for-byte; no new facts, names, numbers, dates, quotes or citations; never alter quoted text; treat multi-word technical terms as atomic (tortured phrases); never strip hyphens; never add dashes; preserve register and tone; no staccato splitting; no stock "human" moves (asides, anecdotes, rhetorical questions) the author did not write; when a fix needs a missing specific, flag it to the user instead of inventing it.
6. *Fix by restating the point, not patching the phrase.* For structural findings the fixer rewrites the sentence or paragraph around its main claim, as Humanizer and the LAMP results suggest; for lexical findings it uses the smallest replacement.
7. *Second pass after the fix.* Re-run a narrow scan for the tells most likely to survive or be newly introduced by a rewrite: contrast frames, restating closers, triads, dashes, bold labels, plus the fixer's own failure signatures (fragment runs, one-sentence paragraph stacks, shrug closers, new clichés replacing old ones, length drop beyond a threshold, missing claims). This addresses whack-a-mole and the "humanizer voice" problem.
8. *Content-preservation check.* Compare claims, numbers, names and quotes between input and output; a large length drop with lost claims is a failure, not a success.
9. *Do not chase detectors.* The success criterion is reader-facing quality; detector scores are decoupled from it.
10. *Name specific patterns, avoid vague goals.* Lane prompts should list concrete patterns with why-it-fails and write-instead notes (Claudisms format), not "make it sound human", which vendor docs show swaps one default for another.

**Candidate lane: `fixer-guard` (contract and second-pass verification, whole document, runs after the fixer)**
- Hunt: introduced facts/numbers/names/quotes not in the source; edited quotations; tortured multi-word terms; hyphen loss; added dashes; runs of 3+ sentence fragments or one-sentence paragraphs; shrug closers; register shift (formal to colloquial or the reverse); total length change beyond about 25% or any dropped claim; surviving strong tells.
- Fix guidance: revert the offending edit to the source sentence and re-attempt the minimal repair, or leave the original and report.
- Keep-list: everything protected in item 4.

**Candidate lane: `vendor-tics` (per passage, focused on response-shaped text)**
- Hunt: flattering or affirming openers ("Great question", "Certainly!", "Absolutely!", "You're absolutely right"); preamble ("Here is...", "Based on..."); sincerity intensifiers ("genuinely", "honestly", "straightforward"); caveat and disclaimer bloat, "as an AI" reminders; "Bottom Line:" or "In short:" summary labels; "Question? Answer." device; unprompted "X, not Y" alternatives; invented hyphenated compound labels; mannered prose (metaphor where a literal phrase exists); over-formatting (bold labels, short-fragment bullets in prose contexts); helpfulness phrasing ("can help you", "is especially helpful").
- Fix guidance: delete openers and preamble and start with the answer; replace metaphor with the literal phrase; fold fragment bullets into sentences when items are not parallel or sequential; collapse caveats to one short sentence placed after the main answer.
- Keep-list: acknowledgement openers when the requested register is warm or support-oriented; lists for genuinely parallel, sequential or comparative items; necessary safety caveats.
- Per-model notes: Claude, sincerity intensifiers, mannered prose, superlatives and helpfulness phrasing, with Opus 5 length bloat; GPT, hyphenated compound labels, "Bottom Line:", corrective "rather than" framing, heavy formatting; Gemini, terse by default, so bloat findings are rarer and intensifiers and formal transitions are more likely.

**Shared fix-guidance notes for all lanes (from tool failure modes)**
- Never delete all adverbs or all hedges; keep a hedge that covers an unverified claim.
- Never apply "two items beat three" mechanically; keep three when the meaning has three parts.
- Refresh lexical lists per model release; keep structural patterns as the stable core.
